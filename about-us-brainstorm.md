## ABOUT PAGE TEMPLATE

**Section 1: Hero / Belief Statement**
- Header: "About [Product Name]"
- Opening line: a bold, values-driven statement of belief — a universal truth about who deserves what, framed as a fairness/access principle (1 sentence)
- Reframe: "[Product] is more than a [category]. It is a [bigger noun — movement/mission/etc.]." State what really matters isn't [surface-level qualifier] but [the true qualifier — effort, readiness, character].
- Tension line: introduce what's currently broken/hidden/inaccessible using an emotionally resonant "but" statement (2 sentences max).
- Close: "We are here to change that." (or equivalent short, resolute pivot line)

**Section 2: The Problem**
- Subheader: "The problem"
- Bold one-line thesis naming the systemic inequity/inefficiency (short, declarative)
- 2-3 sentences building the stakes in ascending order: impact on [primary user], then [secondary user/counterpart], then [society/larger system] — each sentence should escalate the scope of the loss
- Close: "That is why [Product] exists — not just as a [category], but as a [aspirational identity/movement]." Tie to a larger promise (e.g., "no [resource] is wasted").

**Section 3: Our Mission**
- Subheader: "Our mission"
- Bold one-line mission statement: "To make [core value] easy/possible for everyone, [scope qualifier — geography/market]."
- Explain the dual-sided (or multi-sided) value prop: "We are building tools that support both sides of [core exchange]. If you are [user type A], [Product] helps you [benefit]. If you are [user type B], [Product] helps you [benefit]."
- Add a community/identity layer: "We are also building a community of [aspirational label for engaged users] — people who [behavior 1], [behavior 2], and [behavior 3]."

**Section 4: How We Help (Feature Breakdown by Audience)**
- Subheader: "How we help"
- Split into 2+ audience segments (e.g., "For [User Type A]", "For [User Type B]")
- Each segment: 3-4 numbered features (01, 02, 03...)
  - Format per feature: **Short Punchy Feature Name** — one sentence benefit statement, framed as solving a specific pain point ("no more [pain]", "so you never [pain]", "cutting [cost] by [amount]")
  - Mix functional benefit + emotional/efficiency payoff in each line

**Section 5: Our Vision**
- Subheader: "Our vision"
- One aspirational sentence: "To become [scope]'s leading [category] — by making [core process] [3 adjectives, e.g., fast, transparent, fair] for everyone."

**Section 6: What You Can Do Today (Call to Action)**
- Subheader: "What you can do today"
- Intro line: "Whether you are [role A], [role B], or just want to [role C] — there is a place for you here."
- 3 numbered action blocks, one per audience:
  - Role label (e.g., "Job Seekers")
  - One-sentence instruction of what to do + implicit benefit
  - CTA button text (imperative, 2-4 words)
- Include a "everyone/community" action option beyond the core two sides (e.g., referrals, advocacy, sharing)

**Closing line**
- Short, rhythmic, aphoristic sentence that mirrors the mission at a micro scale (e.g., "One step at a time. One opportunity at a time.")

---

### Tone & Style Notes to Pass Along
- Short sentences, declarative, confident — avoid hedging language
- Repetition as rhetorical device (parallel structure: "If you are X... If you are Y...")
- Concrete pain points over abstract claims ("no more endless scrolling" vs. "improved efficiency")
- Each section subheader is lowercase-style, minimal (2-3 words)
- Numbered lists use two-digit format (01, 02, 03)
- Movement/mission language over corporate language — this is a company with a cause, not just a tool
